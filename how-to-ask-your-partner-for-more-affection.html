How to ask your partner for more affection without it sounding like a complaint

Wanting more hugs, touch or kind words is normal. The way you ask decides whether your partner leans in or feels criticized. Here is a warmer way to say it.

Say what you want, not what is missing

"You never touch me anymore" sounds like an attack, even when it comes from a tender place. Try the wish instead: "I would love more hugs during the day."

Pick a calm moment

Right after a fight or in bed at midnight is hard to hear. Choose a quiet time and open softly: "Can I tell you something I have been wanting? It is good, not bad."

Be specific and small

"More affection" is vague. A kiss when you leave, ten minutes on the couch together, a hand on the back while cooking. Small and clear is easier to say yes to.

Say how it makes you feel

Add the feeling behind the request: "When we hug first thing in the morning, I feel close to you all day." That gives your partner a reason, not a rule.

Ask what feels good to them

People show and want love in different ways. Ask: "What makes you feel loved? What would you like more of from me?" Then do one of those things this week.

Notice and thank every attempt

When your partner tries, say so: "That meant a lot." Thanks makes the next try easier. Criticism after an attempt makes people stop trying.
